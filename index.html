⚠ CRITICAL NOTES
  • This tool is a preliminary sizing aid only. It does NOT replace a licensed Texas PE design or a site-specific geotechnical investigation.
  • Effective PI, slope correction, over-consolidation, and fill conditions must come from a soils report. Uncompacted fill under beams will cause failure.
  • Concentrated loads (fireplace, posts, columns, HVAC pads) must be checked separately for local bearing — they are not in the uniform w value.
  • Beam steel shown is per beam at the design spacing. Perimeter bars are continuous; interior beams/bars may stop at 2×Lc past the last needed intersection. Always provide both top and bottom steel plus slab reinforcing.
How to Use This Tool
  1. Enter the overall building length and width in feet.
  2. Enter the Texas ZIP code. The tool estimates a Climatic Rating (Cw) based on broad regional bands.
    • Houston / Gulf Coast / East Texas (77xxx) ≈ 25
    • Dallas / North Central (75xxx–76xxx) ≈ 20–22
    • San Antonio / South Central (78xxx) ≈ 17–18
    • West Texas / Panhandle (79xxx) ≈ 15
    Override Cw directly if you have a site-specific value.
  3. Enter the Effective Plasticity Index from the geotechnical report (15-ft weighted average). This is the single most important soil parameter.
  4. Adjust unit weight if needed (200 psf is conservative for most 1-story wood, brick-veneer, or light-commercial buildings including the slab).
  5. Click Calculate Design. Review the calculated grade-beam depth and slab thickness. Round depths up to practical even inches if desired.
  6. Read top and bottom bars as per beam at the listed spacing. Perimeter cages stay continuous. Interior beams do not have to run the full building length; extend them at least 2×Lc past the last intersection that needs them.

How Beam Spacing Affects Top and Bottom Bars

WRI strip moments ML and MS are the demand for the whole width of the slab in that direction. That demand is shared by every grade beam crossing that width.

Per-beam moment = ML ÷ Nl  (long-span beams) or MS ÷ Ns  (short-span beams).

Bar sizes in Section 5b and in the Design Summary are selected from that per-beam moment. Do not place the strip As into every beam — that is what produced oversized cages such as 5–#7 top and bottom in a 12 in beam.

Perimeter: 2 bars top + 2 bars bottom, continuous, with corner bars.
Interior: same bar size each beam, but the beam and the long bars may be discontinued if they extend at least 2×Lc beyond the last intersection required by the layout (Texas Section ASCE Recommended Practice). Lap 30 bar diameters at splices.

Automatic PI Estimate — Data Basis

When a Texas ZIP is entered, the tool estimates Effective Plasticity Index from major USDA Major Land Resource Areas (MLRAs) and common engineering soil associations used in Texas foundation practice:

  • Gulf Coast Prairie (Houston, Pearland, Brazoria, Galveston) — Beaumont, Bernard, Lake Charles clays → high PI (typically 35–55)
  • Blackland Prairie (Dallas, Waco, parts of Austin) — Houston Black and similar → high PI (28–50)
  • South Texas Plains (San Antonio, Coastal Bend) — mixed clay / gravelly / limestone residual → moderate (18–40)
  • East Texas Timberlands — sandy loams over clay → moderate (18–40)
  • High Plains / West Texas — silty, sandy, caliche → lower PI (10–30)

Important: These are regional averages for preliminary sizing only. USDA Web Soil Survey (SSURGO) map-unit data and a site-specific geotechnical investigation (15-ft weighted Effective PI) are required for final PE-sealed design. Local fill, slope, and drainage can change the design significantly.

Residential & Light Commercial Coordination Notes
  • Use the overall foundation envelope, or split L-shapes / porches / garages into overlapping rectangles and take the stricter beam depth.
  • Stone veneer, porch posts, fireplaces, and column reactions are line or point loads — keep them on the grade beams; do not hide them only in w.
  • Perimeter beams with a brick / stone ledge need the extra width shown on the architectural foundation sheet (often 3½″–5½″).
  • Garage overhead-door blockouts: do not cut the bottom bars; add extra bars around the opening.
